FOIPPA Section 30.1 was repealed. The rule you think you have is gone.

Ask most BC municipal administrators what FOIPPA requires of an AI tool and you will hear that citizen data has to stay in Canada. That was s. 30.1 — and it was repealed on 25 November 2021 by Bill 22 (SBC 2021, c. 39, s. 17). The consolidated Act now reads “30.1–30.2 [Repealed 2021-39-17.]”, and the words “outside Canada” appear nowhere in it. British Columbia has no data-residency requirement. What it does have is an obligation almost nobody is talking about: since 1 February 2023, s. 36.2 has required the head of every public body — municipalities included — to develop a privacy management program. If your staff are using AI on personal information, that is the gap, not where the server sits.

FOIPPA
Freedom of Information and Protection of Privacy Act (BC)
Governs access to records and protection of personal information held by BC public bodies. A municipality is a “local government body” and therefore a local public body under Schedule 1. Since 1 February 2023, s. 36.2 requires the head of a public body to develop a privacy management program in accordance with the minister’s directions, and s. 36.3 makes privacy breach notification mandatory. Section 30 still requires reasonable security arrangements. Sections 30.1–30.2 — the old residency rule — are repealed.
OIPC BC
Office of the Information and Privacy Commissioner for BC
BC's independent oversight body. Investigates complaints, audits public bodies, and issues orders. Has increasingly focused on cloud services and AI data handling. Municipalities that cannot demonstrate policy and training are at meaningful risk.
UBCM
Union of BC Municipalities
Has identified AI governance and data privacy as active priorities for BC local government in 2025–2026. Spencer Morley Consulting's Municipal AI Framework addresses the specific FOIPPA requirements that UBCM guidance references.
PIA
Privacy Impact Assessment
Under s. 69, a privacy impact assessment determines whether a current or proposed system, project, program or activity meets the requirements of Part 3 of FIPPA. A BC municipality standing up any new AI tool — including a private LLM — is squarely in that definition. Spencer Morley Consulting supports PIA preparation as part of the policy and deployment work.

BC Municipality FAQ

Questions from BC CAOs and municipal administrators

Doesn’t FOIPPA require citizen data to stay in Canada?

Not any more, and this is the single most common misunderstanding in BC local government. Section 30.1 did require it — and it was repealed on 25 November 2021 by Bill 22 (SBC 2021, c. 39, s. 17). The consolidated Act now shows ss. 30.1–30.2 as repealed, and the phrase “outside Canada” does not appear in it at all. What still applies: s. 30 (reasonable security arrangements), s. 36.2 (a privacy management program, required since 1 February 2023), s. 36.3 (mandatory breach notification) and s. 69 (privacy impact assessments). Where a tool sends data is still a real security question — it is just not a prohibition, and a policy built on the wrong section will not survive scrutiny.

Our municipality already uses Microsoft 365 with Copilot. Are we compliant with FOIPPA?

Compliance here is not a question about where the data sits. Since s. 30.1 was repealed, a Canadian data centre is not what makes Copilot compliant and a US one is not what makes it non-compliant. The questions that decide it: do you have the privacy management program s. 36.2 has required since February 2023; was a PIA completed under s. 69 before deployment; are the security arrangements reasonable under s. 30; and do staff know what they may and may not put into it. The AI Readiness Assessment establishes what is actually in use and which of those are missing.

We're a small BC municipality — District of X with 3 staff. Is this service appropriate for us?

Yes — small municipalities are exactly who this service is built for. Large BC municipalities (Metro Vancouver member cities, City of Victoria, etc.) have legal counsel and dedicated privacy officers. Small district municipalities, villages, improvement districts, and rural communities typically don't — and generic enterprise-level compliance templates don't work for a three-person office.
