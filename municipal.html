03
AI Adoption Policy & Procedures
Two documents doing two different jobs: the policy council adopts, and the procedures administration runs on.
A policy sets the rules. It is the document council votes on, the one you produce for a provincial oversight body, and the one that states what staff may and may not do with AI. It is necessary — and on its own it is not sufficient. Policies get adopted and then sit in a binder, because nobody wrote down what following them looks like on a Tuesday afternoon when a staff member has a tool open and a deadline.
So Spencer Morley Consulting writes both. The AI Adoption Policy is the council-facing instrument: purpose and scope, sanctioned and prohibited uses, data handling grounded in your province’s privacy legislation, breach reporting, assigned accountability, and mandatory training — in language a council can read, question, and approve.
The AI Adoption Procedures are the administrative companion: how a staff member requests a tool and who approves it, what the review actually checks, what gets logged and where, the exact steps the moment someone suspects personal information went into a public tool, and how the sanctioned tool list is kept current as the market moves.
Delivered as two separate documents on purpose. Council adopts the policy without having to approve every operational detail, and administration can revise the procedures as tools change without returning to council each time.
Written to your province — ATIA and POPA in Alberta, FOIPPA in BC, MFIPPA in Ontario, FIPPA in Manitoba, LAFOIP in Saskatchewan. Pricing on request.

04
Privacy Management Program Integration
Your AI policy belongs inside the program the legislation already requires — not beside it.
Alberta public bodies were required to have a Privacy Management Program established and implemented by June 11, 2026 under POPA s. 25. Many municipalities have one. Far fewer have one that accounts for the AI their staff had already started using.
That matters because the contents of the program are prescribed by regulation. The Protection of Privacy (Ministerial) Regulation, Alta Reg 143/2025, s. 6(1)(b)(iii) requires policies and procedures for how automated systems will use personal information; s. 6(2)(a)(v) adds policies on the use of personal information in artificial intelligence systems for public bodies holding high volumes of personal information or highly sensitive personal information. The AI element is not an addition to the program — it is part of what the program was always required to contain.
An AI policy drafted as a free-standing document leaves a municipality with two instruments that name different officers, define terms differently, and route approvals along different paths — and a program that still has the same hole in it.
This engagement closes that. We read your existing privacy policy, your program and your privacy impact assessment process; map the prescribed s. 6 contents against what you already hold; write the AI policy and procedures as components that cross-reference your privacy officer designation, security classification system, breach response and PIA triggers; and prepare the amendment and adoption package that puts them formally into the program.
Outside Alberta the same logic applies under the equivalent provincial regime. Pricing on request.
