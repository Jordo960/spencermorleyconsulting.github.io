Crisis Communications & Recovery Municipality of Jasper, Alberta · 2024 Wildfire Response & Recovery

Two and a Half Months on the Ground: Jasper's 2024 Wildfire Recovery.

The Situation
The July 2024 Jasper wildfire forced a community-wide evacuation lasting roughly five weeks, with visitor access to the area restricted for a further four weeks after residents were allowed back (Destination Think). Recovery efforts were later formalized through the Jasper Recovery Coordination Centre, a partnership between the Municipality of Jasper and Parks Canada established August 22, 2024 to coordinate interim housing, debris management, rebuilding, and social and economic recovery (Municipality of Jasper). It was a national news story unfolding in real time, with residents, businesses, and all three levels of government needing coordinated, accurate information at every stage.
The Scope
Cheryl Spencer-Morley served as Lead Crisis Communications Consultant for the Jasper wildfires, spending roughly two and a half months on the ground supporting the municipality's communications with residents, local businesses, and recovery partners — messaging on re-entry, revitalizing the visitor economy, debris removal coordination, and housing support initiatives (CatIQ Connect speaker profile). The mandate also covered government relations, recovery planning, stakeholder relations, and council communications throughout the response and recovery period.
The Approach
Rather than advising remotely, Cheryl embedded directly with the municipality — effectively serving as its Public Information Officer and communications liaison for the duration of the deployment, including day-to-day management of the municipality's social media and website updates alongside her advisory work. That on-the-ground presence meant messaging to residents, businesses, council, and recovery partners could stay consistent even as the situation changed week to week.
Delivered
  • Roughly two and a half months embedded on the ground as Lead Crisis Communications Consultant for the Jasper wildfires — independently corroborated by CatIQ Connect's speaker profile.
  • Communications support across re-entry messaging, revitalizing the visitor economy, debris removal coordination, and housing support initiatives.
  • Government relations, recovery planning, stakeholder relations, and council communications throughout the response and recovery period.
  • Functioned as the municipality's de facto Public Information Officer and communications liaison, including hands-on social media and website management.
  • Invited panelist, "Navigating the Jasper Fires: Perspectives on Planning, Communications and the Role of NGOs," CatIQ Connect 2025 — Canada's national catastrophe conference (Feb. 5, 2025).

AI & Municipal Consulting Municipalities · Alberta

Municipal Work That Comes From Actually Working With Municipalities.

The Background
Jordan Morley's municipal work has involved building online resident survey programs and preparing structured reports based on community data analysis for Alberta municipalities. That work — understanding how municipal staff operate, what data they work with, and how councils receive and act on information — directly informs the AI consulting practice.
What That Work Taught
Municipal teams are small. Administrators carry multiple files simultaneously. Councils need plain-language outputs they can act on without technical background. Residents expect clear communication about how their input is used. These aren't abstract principles — they're what Jordan has navigated in practice, and they shape every aspect of how SMC's Municipal AI Framework is designed and delivered.
The AI Consulting Connection
The AI consulting practice builds directly on this foundation. The same municipalities that need help with resident engagement and data analysis are now facing AI governance obligations under ATIA, POPA, FOIPPA, and MFIPPA. Jordan's familiarity with how small municipal teams actually work — and what they can realistically implement — is what makes the Municipal AI Framework practical rather than aspirational.

Digital Brand Launch Development Advisory · Edmonton, Alberta · Complete

Full Digital Brand Launch for a New Alberta Development Advisory Firm.

The Situation
Two experienced advisors were incorporating a new development advisory firm in Alberta, working with housing developers and nonprofits on government relations, regulatory strategy, permitting and funding. They had deep professional expertise and a clear positioning — but no brand, no website, no domain, no business email, and no digital presence of any kind. They needed everything built from scratch before their first client conversations.
The Scope
SMC was engaged for a full digital brand launch: brand strategy and identity (logo, colour palette, typography), domain registration and Google Workspace setup, a custom website with AI-era visibility built in from the start (SEO, AEO, GEO, WebMCP), and a LinkedIn company presence. A coming-soon page was deployed within weeks of engagement start to establish the domain while the full site was built.
The Approach
A discovery call established audience, tone, and design direction. The brand mark was designed around the client's core concept — the golden ratio — using a sage-olive palette informed by cultural sensitivity research specific to the Alberta Treaty region. The website was built in Next.js with a full structured data and AI-visibility layer (JSON-LD, llms.txt, sitemap, OG/Twitter) verified in static export before deployment to Cloudflare Pages.
Delivered to Date
  • Brand strategy and identity: positioning brief, custom brand mark, colour palette, typography system, motion parameters.
  • Domain registered and Google Workspace configured — three paid seats plus a shared group alias. Operational email before the website launched.
  • Coming-soon page deployed to Cloudflare Pages while the full site was built — domain live and claiming search presence from day one.
  • Full Next.js website built with SEO, AEO, GEO, and WebMCP implementation: metadata, canonical URLs, Organization JSON-LD, sitemap, robots.txt, and llms.txt. Build verified across all 13 routes.
  • Contact form wired to a third-party form service — no back-end infrastructure required.
  • Cultural sensitivity review completed on brand colour — desk research across Alberta treaty territories, reviewing the palette against the cultural traditions of Plains Cree, Blackfoot, Tsuut'ina, Stoney Nakoda, and Métis communities.
  • The website has been live at ratiostrategy.ca since June 2026, and the LinkedIn company page is live with both principals as administrators.
