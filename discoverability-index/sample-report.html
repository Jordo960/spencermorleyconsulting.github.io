01

Executive summary

Ask Perplexity, ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, or Claude who to call for artificial turf in Edmonton and the company almost never comes up. Across 8 commercial buyer questions put to all five engines in August 2026 (38 of 40 possible answers captured live, 2 marked not captured rather than guessed), the company was named in 7, and in one of those, Gemini merged the company into a competitor’s listing rather than naming it on its own.

That absence is the story of this report. The company scores 35 of 100 on the SMC Discoverability Index: 8th (tied) of the 9 businesses scored, level with Parkland Landscape Centre, whose website carries a banner saying it is no longer monitored. The gap to the leader, Summit Turf Works at 68.5, is wide. The most damaging single finding: on the direct question "Is VerdeLawn good artificial turf?", Perplexity, Gemini, and Copilot all credit Prairie Synthetic Grass as VerdeLawn’s exclusive supplier, ChatGPT credits no company at all, and Claude declines to name a supplier rather than guess. The company (the actual business behind the product) received zero attribution across all five engines.

The board is more winnable than the rank suggests. Every business above the company has an exposed flank: the AI-visibility leader blocks browsers at its own front door with a security challenge; the review-volume leader runs a dead website; the top homeowner installer hasn't claimed its Google profile; the putting-green specialist has one Google review. The company's foundations (a 5.0★ profile, clean on-page basics, and a working AI-agent endpoint most competitors lack) are real. What's missing is the entity layer, the content, and the third-party footprint that AI engines build their answers from. All three are buildable.

02

What we looked at

Every business was measured the same way, live, on 2026-08-13: browser-rendered page inspection (not raw scraping, which JS-built sites defeat), technical probes of each domain, a Jordan-approved set of 14 buyer queries (the eight AI-intent queries run as fresh conversations across five AI engines (Perplexity, ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, and Claude), and Canada-weighted trust checks on the platforms that matter for trades), Google Business Profile, HomeStars, TrustedPros, and Consumer Choice.

We measured 4 layers of discoverability. Classical SEO: what search engines can read, index, and rank. AEO / GEO: Answer and Generative Engine Optimisation, how a business appears when buyers ask ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Copilot, or Claude for recommendations in Edmonton–Calgary. webMCP readiness: whether a site publishes data in formats AI agents can parse directly. The fourth pillar, Canadian trust signals, scores the review and directory presence Canadian buyers (and the engines that cite them) actually use.

03

The company today

3.1 Classical SEO 15.5 / 30

A 3-page site cannot answer a single buyer question

The company's website is home, about, and a quote form. No pricing page, no FAQ, no VerdeLawn™ product page, no wholesale-program page, no city pages. Every cost, spec, and "best of" question buyers ask goes to competitors' content instead. This one gap suppresses two pillars at once. Fix horizon: 30–60 days.

On-page basics are clean

Live render: strong keyword title ("Wholesale Artificial Turf Supplier Alberta & Installation"), a real meta description, exactly one H1, sane heading order, all images carrying alt text. Two competitors above the company on the board (GreenSpan Surfaces, Northgate Grass) render with zero H1s. The plumbing works; there's just very little flowing through it.

Fresh sitemap, thin authority

Sitemap valid and current (lastmod 2026-07-04) with clean robots.txt. But the citation footprint is the site itself, the Google profile, and Instagram: no directories, no third-party mentions found. Domain authority signals score 2/6 [estimated].

3.2 Answer & generative engine optimisation (AEO / GEO) 8.5 / 35

VerdeLawn™ is being credited to a competitor on three of five engines

Asked "Is VerdeLawn good artificial turf?", Perplexity, Gemini, and Copilot all name Prairie Synthetic Grass as VerdeLawn’s exclusive supplier/dealer; ChatGPT credits no company at all; Claude, alone among the five, declines to name a supplier rather than assert one. Gemini’s wholesale answer goes further, listing "Prairie Synthetic Grass / the company" as one merged entity and handing the combined listing the company’s product exclusivity. The company’s flagship asset is doing marketing work for someone else on three engines out of five. Fix horizon: 30–90 days (schema + product page + citations).

Named in 7 of 38 captured commercial AI answers

The seven: first-named on Perplexity’s, Copilot’s, and Claude’s wholesale-supplier answers, plus mentioned on ChatGPT’s; a top-3 "call first" spot on ChatGPT’s bulk-Calgary answer; first-named on Claude’s homeowner-installer answer, plus a secondary "also worth considering" mention on Perplexity’s. Absent from all 8 cost and winter-spec answers reached (2 Claude cells on those queries were not captured), all 5 putting-green answers, and all 5 review-volume answers. Prairie Synthetic Grass was named first in 11 of 38 (the most of any business measured); AllSeason Greens in 5, all on the putting-green query it owns outright.

Zero presence on the sources engines cite

ChatGPT and Copilot both built their installer shortlists from third-party sources: listicles, directories, review aggregators. The company appears on none of them: not a national "best of" listicle, not an Edmonton vendor roundup (both live and [2026]-dated), not HomeStars, not TrustedPros. When engines cited the company at all, the only source was the company’s own website.

A 5.0★ profile without volume, or an address

The company’s Google Business Profile is rated 5.0★ across 23 reviews with owner responses and fresh photos, but shows no street address (service-area setup), and the review count trails Parkland Landscape Centre (386), Summit Turf Works (179), and GreenSpan Surfaces (85). Volume is what earns a spot in the Places results engines quote, and it’s explicit, not assumed: asked to explain how it ranks Edmonton turf installers, Copilot named review volume and star rating as its first two criteria, then led with Parkland Landscape Centre. No HomeStars, TrustedPros, or Consumer Choice presence, though notably, nobody in this set holds those either (section 07).

How the engines describe the company diagnostic, not scored

Asked about the company by name, the engines that could be reached get the facts right. This is not a factual-accuracy problem. It’s a category problem: what the engines reach for when nobody has told them who the company is.

Correct by name, conflated by category

Asked directly about the company, Perplexity, Gemini, and ChatGPT all report the founding year, service area, and VerdeLawn™ relationship correctly. The same three engines still misattribute VerdeLawn™ supply to a competitor when the question is framed as a buyer question instead of an identity question. The facts are right; the entity graph that would keep them attached to the right business in every context is missing. Fix horizon: same schema work as the item above.

Claude hedges instead of guessing: worth noting, not yet worth relying on

On the VerdeLawn™ supplier question, Claude is the only one of the five that declines to name a business rather than assert an answer without a confident source. That’s a better failure mode than the other four engines’ confident wrong answer, but it’s still an absence. A hedge doesn’t send a buyer anywhere; a wrong confident answer sends them to a competitor. Both cost the same job.

3.3 webMCP readiness 6.5 / 20

One schema block: "WebSite." That's the whole entity layer.

Live render found a single JSON-LD block of type WebSite: no LocalBusiness, no Service or Product, no FAQPage, no AggregateRating. Address, phone, and email exist only in the page footer, not in structured data. This is the single biggest fixable technical gap, and it feeds the Gemini identity confusion directly: engines have no machine-readable statement of who the company is and what it owns. Fix horizon: 2–4 weeks.

A real AI-agent head start, inherited from the site platform

The company's website publishes a live llms.txt describing the business, VerdeLawn™, and service area, and documents a working site MCP endpoint (/_api/mcp) with tools for business details, site search, and booking/purchase flows. Only one competitor of eight (Summit Turf Works, via its commerce platform) has anything comparable; six have nothing at all. Sharpen it and the company is ahead of 7 of 8 competitors on the machine-readable web.

04

The Edmonton–Calgary competitive landscape

Eight competitors were scored with identical method. What the board shows: every business ahead of the company is winning on one or two pillars while leaving another wide open. Nobody in this market is strong everywhere, which is what makes the climb realistic.

Summit Turf Works (SDI 68.5, rank 1)

The only competitor treating AI as a channel: substantive llms.txt, a live agentic-commerce endpoint (agents can query the catalog and transact today), 4.9★ across 179 Google reviews, #1 on an Edmonton vendor roundup's turf list, and #1 answers on wholesale and winter-spec queries. The flank: it's Calgary-anchored wholesale, thin on Edmonton homeowner installs, no HomeStars/TrustedPros, and its entity schema is generic.

Prairie Synthetic Grass (SDI 61, rank 2)

The AI-visibility leader: named first in 11 of the 38 captured answers, more than any other business measured, plus both live Edmonton listicles, a HomeStars profile, 4.5★/49, and currently the credited owner of the company’s VerdeLawn™ story on three of five engines. Two flanks: its site met our live browser with a security challenge twice (on-page code [unverified], a wall against the AI crawlers it invites in robots.txt), and its VerdeLawn claim is contestable by the company that actually holds the line.

GreenSpan Surfaces (SDI 48, rank 3)

Perplexity's #1 homeowner recommendation, 4.9★/85, a 5.0 Houzz profile, LocalBusiness + FAQPage schema. The flanks: its Google profile is literally unclaimed ("Claim this business"), the homepage renders zero H1s, and turf is one service page among a dozen. It wins as a landscaper, not a turf specialist.

Cascade Outdoor Surfaces (SDI 47.5, rank 4)

Rides the Meridian Turf dealer network: the brand appears in 14 of 38 captured answers, Cascade Outdoor Surfaces itself in only 2. Fresh site, rich schema graph, 4.6★/43. Its visibility is rented from a brand it doesn’t own, the same play the company can run with VerdeLawn™, as owner rather than tenant.

AllSeason Greens (SDI 45.5, rank 5)

Owns putting-green intent outright: #1 on all five engines for that query, on content depth and the global brand’s proof (a pro-golf brand partnership, published green-speed ratings). The flank is stark: one Google review, an out-of-town address, no listicle or directory presence. Its moat is content, the most copyable kind.

TrueLine Turf (SDI 41.5) · Northgate Grass (SDI 37.5) · Parkland Landscape Centre (SDI 35)

TrueLine Turf: solid AI mentions, 8 reviews split across two Google listings, blog stale since 2017. Northgate Grass: national content earns AI citations, but its Edmonton profile has zero reviews and no website link, plus zero H1s and 40 of 42 images missing alt text. Parkland Landscape Centre: the market's review giant (4.7★/386) behind an abandoned website, proof that review equity alone no longer converts to AI recommendations.

What the gap costs

We model cost on recovered jobs, not traffic estimates: the arithmetic a buyer can check. Where the company already wins

The rank is low; the raw material isn't. These are the assets the roadmap builds on, several of them advantages nobody else in the set holds.

  • First-named on three of five engines for wholesale supply. Asked for the best wholesale turf suppliers in Alberta, Perplexity, Copilot, and Claude all put the company first, with a dedicated contractor-program description; ChatGPT mentioned it as well. The positioning engines reward is already proven, across most of the engine set, on one track.
  • A perfect rating with real reviews. 5.0★ across 23 Google reviews, owner responses, photos updated within the month. Nobody above the company has a flawless rating at that volume.
  • VerdeLawn™: the product engines already talk about. The brand appears across AI answers today; the attribution is wrong, but the demand signal is real and the company owns the product. Reclaiming an existing story is faster than building one.
  • An agentic endpoint 7 of 8 competitors lack. Live llms.txt plus a working site MCP endpoint with booking/purchase tools. Only Summit Turf Works matches this, and only at the product-catalog level.
  • Clean technical basics. One H1, full alt text, fresh sitemap, while two higher-ranked competitors render broken heading structures and one runs a dead site.
  • Open ground on the trust pillar. Nobody in the set is on TrustedPros; nobody holds the 2026 Northern Alberta Consumer Choice award for artificial grass (it went to a supplies yard outside this set). First mover takes these uncontested.

09

The 90-day picture

Items 1–6 are all within the company's direct control, no waiting on third parties. Completed, they plausibly move the SDI from 35 into the low 50s [estimated]: roughly +6 on webMCP (entity schema), +4–5 on classical SEO (content depth), and +3–4 on Canadian trust (directories, profile completion). On the current board that's top-4 territory (past Parkland Landscape Centre, Northgate Grass, TrueLine Turf, AllSeason Greens, and Cascade Outdoor Surfaces) with the third-party citation work (item 7) still compounding behind it.

The realistic route to #1 runs through the two leaders' weaknesses. Summit Turf Works owns wholesale AI answers but is Calgary-anchored and invisible on Edmonton homeowner intent. The company can take the Edmonton-installer answers Summit Turf Works doesn't contest. Prairie Synthetic Grass owns overall AI visibility but is renting its best story from the company: the moment engines have a machine-readable, third-party-corroborated statement that the company is VerdeLawn™'s supplier, the misattribution that props up its Q8 presence starts working in reverse. Neither leader holds a review rating as strong as the company's, and neither holds the trades directories or the Consumer Choice award, because nobody does yet.

AI answers refresh on their own cadence, so changes land on a lag. We recommend a re-capture of the full query set at 6–8 weeks (mid-October 2026) to measure movement, then the quarterly re-score to hold the trajectory. The first checkpoint that matters: the company named (correctly, on its own) in the VerdeLawn™ answer on at least three of five engines.
