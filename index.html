A few useful answers.

Who leads the work?

Cheryl Spencer-Morley leads the crisis communications practice at Spencer Morley Consulting. Jordan Morley leads the AI consulting practice at Spencer Morley Consulting.

What is the difference between a policy and procedures?

The policy states the rules and who is accountable for them; it is what council or leadership formally adopts. The procedures state what people actually do: how a tool gets approved, what is logged, and what happens when something goes wrong.

What does a crisis communications consultant do for a municipality?

Most of the work happens before anything goes wrong: a current crisis communications plan with clear roles and approval chains, prepared holding statements, media training for spokespeople and elected officials, and tabletop exercises that test the plan against Alberta Emergency Management Act requirements. When an incident is live, the consultant supports the emergency operations centre and the public information function: message development, media relations, and regular updates to council, residents, and staff. After the incident, the work shifts to recovery communications, which can run for months. For the County of Grande Prairie No. 1, SMC redeveloped the crisis communications plan, designed a new internal communications plan, and trained staff to run both.

When should a municipality bring in outside crisis communications support?

Before an incident: when the crisis communications plan is several years old, has never been exercised, or depends on one person. During an incident: when it runs past the first few days, when your communications lead is also covering other emergency roles, or when the position is vacant. Outside support is easiest to add when the plan already names it, so the best time to arrange it is before you need it. If an incident is live now, call 780-236-3332.
