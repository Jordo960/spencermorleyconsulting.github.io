Example scenario walkthrough

What a stress test finds before your council sees the draft.

A policy that reads well can still fail the first time someone has to apply it. Between the first and second draft, every AI adoption policy we write is run against written case scenarios. This is what that produced on one municipal engagement.

How the test is run

The documents are used, not read.

Each scenario is written as though the policy and its procedures had already been adopted and were in force, then walked through the way a CAO or privacy officer would actually use them: which provision is engaged, in what order, who must act, what happens next, and what the documents do not say. Every clause relied on is quoted before it is relied on. Where a draft leans on another instrument — the privacy policy, the acceptable-use policy, an HR policy — that instrument is read too, so a cross-reference is never taken on trust.

Three rules govern the exercise. No problem is manufactured: where the documents handle a situation, the result says so. Every fix is written as replacement or additional text that keeps the existing clause numbers, so nothing has to be renumbered. And where a gap sits outside what a municipal policy can fix — a provincial sanction regime that does not yet exist, for example — the result says that plainly rather than proposing a clause that would overreach.

The pattern was consistent.

The drafts were strong at saying what is and is not permitted. Every prohibition, every characterisation and every reporting route landed correctly, and in three places they handled a hard case better than they had to.

Where they were thinner is in saying who acts next — after a report reaches the head of council, when a vendor feature must be switched off, when a collection notice on a form is wrong, when a high-risk activity recurs daily. That is the shape of the thing a stress test finds and a careful read does not.

Verdict — handled, fix proposed.

Every rule, characterisation and reporting route lands, and nobody has to invent a process. Nothing here turns on where the tool’s servers are: the failure is the disclosure to a service the municipality does not control — no agreement, no safeguards, no authority to disclose — and the privacy policy’s breach process is built for exactly that.

The two dashed boxes are the gaps. The training and acknowledgment gates miss the very employee this scenario describes, and the directors’ row does not name the reporting duty. Both were wording changes for the second draft, not changes of rule.

Where this sits

Between the first draft and the second.

The stress test is not a separate product. It runs between phase four and phase five of the municipal method, after the two documents are drafted and before administration and council review them — which is the last point at which changing the wording costs nothing.
