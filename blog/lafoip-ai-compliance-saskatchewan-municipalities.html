AI Compliance for Saskatchewan Municipalities.
What LAFOIP Requires.

LAFOIP is purpose-built for Saskatchewan local authorities — and it governs every AI tool your staff is already using. Here's what the IPC Saskatchewan is watching for, and what your municipality needs to address now.

The Legislation

LAFOIP — Built Specifically for Local Authorities

Saskatchewan is unique among Canadian provinces in having legislation purpose-built for local authorities. The Local Authority Freedom of Information and Protection of Privacy Act (LAFOIP) governs municipalities, school divisions, regional health authorities, and other local public bodies in Saskatchewan — distinct from FOIP, which governs provincial government bodies.

LAFOIP requires Saskatchewan local authorities to have documented policies and procedures governing how personal information is collected, used, disclosed, retained, and protected. That obligation applies regardless of the technology involved. AI tools do not create an exemption from LAFOIP — they create new vectors for non-compliance.

Key distinction: Saskatchewan municipalities are subject to LAFOIP — not FOIP. LAFOIP was written with local government realities in mind. The IPC Saskatchewan has jurisdiction over complaints involving local authority compliance with LAFOIP, and has authority to issue binding orders.

The Information and Privacy Commissioner of Saskatchewan (IPC Saskatchewan) oversees LAFOIP compliance for local authorities. Unlike the Manitoba Ombudsman — a general-purpose oversight body — the IPC Saskatchewan is a dedicated privacy commissioner with specialized expertise, active guidance programs, and enforcement authority that includes binding orders, not just recommendations.

The Compliance Gap

Shadow AI is Already in Your Saskatchewan Municipality.

KPMG Canada's 2025 research found that 48% of Canadian public servants already use AI tools on the job — but only 22% of organizations have a formal AI adoption policy. Saskatchewan municipalities are not exempt from this pattern.

Staff across municipal departments are using ChatGPT, Google Gemini, and Microsoft Copilot in their daily work — for drafting correspondence, summarizing documents, answering questions, and processing information. Most are doing so without any formal governance in place. That is shadow AI, and it creates direct LAFOIP exposure.

48% of Canadian public servants already use AI tools on the job. Only 22% of organizations have a formal AI adoption policy. — KPMG Canada, 2025

Under LAFOIP, entering personal information — ratepayer data, complaint records, bylaw enforcement files, staff information — into a public AI platform without authorization and governance is unauthorized use or disclosure of personal information. A complaint to the IPC Saskatchewan does not require a data breach in the traditional sense. The act of processing personal information through an ungoverned AI tool may itself constitute the violation.

SARM and SUMA

Two Associations. One Compliance Obligation.

Saskatchewan's municipal landscape is served by two major associations: the Saskatchewan Association of Rural Municipalities (SARM) for rural municipalities, and the Saskatchewan Urban Municipalities Association (SUMA) for urban municipalities. Both have acknowledged the importance of AI governance for their members.

Both SARM and SUMA provide resources and guidance for member municipalities — but neither can do the compliance work for you. Privacy governance under LAFOIP must reflect your municipality's specific systems, staff, data practices, and AI use patterns. A generic template from an association is a starting point, not a compliant Privacy Management Program.

Association guidance is not a substitute for a municipality-specific program. The IPC Saskatchewan investigates individual complaints against individual local authorities. Whether your municipality's program is adequate is a question about your municipality — not whether you followed general association guidance.

What You Need to Build

The Core Compliance Components for Saskatchewan Local Authorities

The following checklist represents the foundational governance that a Saskatchewan local authority needs to address AI-related LAFOIP exposure. Larger municipalities may have additional requirements; this covers the minimum necessary for small and rural municipalities.

  • Designate a Privacy Officer

    Formally designate a staff member as Privacy Officer in writing. This person is responsible for access requests, breach management, and maintaining the privacy governance program. Document the designation in a council or administrative resolution.

  • Conduct a Personal Information Inventory

    Map every location where your municipality collects, uses, or discloses personal information — including AI tools staff are currently using. Shadow AI tools belong in this inventory regardless of whether they are officially sanctioned.

  • Draft an AI Acceptable Use Policy

    Create a formal policy governing staff use of AI tools under LAFOIP. Specify sanctioned tools, prohibited tools, data entry restrictions, and consequences for unauthorized use. This is the document that directly addresses shadow AI.

  • Conduct Privacy Impact Assessments for New AI Tools

    Before deploying any new AI tool that processes personal information — including Microsoft Copilot integrated into 365 — conduct and document a Privacy Impact Assessment. The IPC Saskatchewan expects PIAs for new technology that handles personal information.

  • Establish Breach Response Procedures

    Document the steps your municipality will follow in the event of a privacy breach. LAFOIP requires notification to affected individuals and the IPC Saskatchewan for certain breaches. Document who is notified, in what timeframe, and what records are kept.

  • Deliver and Document Staff Training

    Provide privacy and AI governance training to all staff who handle personal information. Keep records of who was trained, when, and on what. Training must be documented — it is a component of your governance program, not just a best practice.

  • Review Vendor and Cloud Agreements

    Review every cloud service and vendor relationship involving personal information. Confirm data processing agreements are in place. AI vendors that process citizen data must have executed agreements that meet LAFOIP requirements.

  • Document and Maintain the Program

    Compile all of the above into a single governance document. Date it, sign it, bring it to council. Establish a review cycle — at minimum annually, or whenever your AI tool stack changes significantly.

Frequently Asked Questions

Saskatchewan LAFOIP and AI — Common Questions

What is LAFOIP and does it apply to Saskatchewan municipalities?

LAFOIP (Local Authority Freedom of Information and Protection of Privacy Act) is Saskatchewan's privacy legislation purpose-built for local authorities — including municipalities, school divisions, and regional health authorities. It applies to all Saskatchewan local authorities and governs how personal information is collected, used, disclosed, retained, and protected.

What is the difference between LAFOIP and FOIP?

LAFOIP governs local authorities in Saskatchewan — municipalities, school divisions, and similar local public bodies. FOIP (Freedom of Information and Protection of Privacy Act) governs provincial government institutions in Saskatchewan. Saskatchewan municipalities are subject to LAFOIP, not FOIP.

Who oversees LAFOIP compliance?

The Information and Privacy Commissioner of Saskatchewan (IPC Saskatchewan) investigates complaints and issues guidance under LAFOIP. Unlike the Manitoba Ombudsman, the IPC Saskatchewan is a dedicated privacy commissioner with authority to issue binding orders — not just recommendations. A finding against your municipality is not advisory.

Is there a LAFOIP deadline for AI governance?

There is no single hard deadline under LAFOIP equivalent to Alberta's ATIA/POPA Privacy Management Program deadline. However, LAFOIP's obligations are ongoing and active. A complaint about unauthorized AI use of citizen data does not require a missed deadline to succeed. The risk is present now.

Microsoft Copilot is appearing in our 365 environment. Do we need to act?

Yes. Microsoft Copilot integrated into 365 interacts with municipal data — including emails, documents, meeting records, and files that may contain citizen personal information. Before deploying Copilot in a production environment, LAFOIP expects a Privacy Impact Assessment documenting what data Copilot accesses, how it is used, where it is stored, and what controls are in place.
