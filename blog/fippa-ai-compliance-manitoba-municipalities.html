AI Compliance for Manitoba Municipalities.
What FIPPA Actually Requires.

The Legislation

FIPPA Part 2 — What Local Public Bodies Must Do

Manitoba's Freedom of Information and Protection of Privacy Act (FIPPA) governs how local public bodies — including municipalities, school divisions, and other local government bodies — collect, use, disclose, retain, and dispose of personal information.

Part 2 of FIPPA applies specifically to local public bodies. Under FIPPA, every local public body must have documented policies and procedures governing the handling of personal information. That obligation does not disappear when the information passes through an AI system. If staff are using ChatGPT, Microsoft Copilot, or any other AI platform in the course of their work — even informally — FIPPA applies.

Key obligation: FIPPA requires local public bodies to protect personal information in their custody or under their control. Personal information entered into a public AI tool is personal information that has left your custody — without authorization, without documentation, and potentially without the individual's knowledge.

Manitoba's privacy framework does not include a dedicated Information and Privacy Commissioner. The Manitoba Ombudsman handles privacy complaints under FIPPA for local public bodies. This is distinct from every other province covered here — and it shapes how compliance enforcement works in Manitoba.

The Oversight Context

The Manitoba Ombudsman — Not an IPC

Every other province covered by Spencer Morley Consulting's municipal practice has a dedicated Information and Privacy Commissioner: the OIPC in Alberta and BC, the IPC in Ontario and Saskatchewan. Manitoba does not.

The Manitoba Ombudsman investigates privacy complaints under FIPPA for local public bodies. The Ombudsman has broad investigative powers — it can investigate complaints, issue recommendations, and report findings publicly. While the Ombudsman's recommendations are not automatically binding orders, a public report finding a municipality in violation of FIPPA has significant reputational and political consequences.

Practically, this means the complaint pathway in Manitoba runs through a general-purpose oversight body rather than a privacy specialist. The Ombudsman's staff investigate across a wide range of public sector issues, and privacy complaints related to AI use are a newer category — but the jurisdiction is clear, and the pathway for a citizen complaint is straightforward.

No deadline does not mean no risk. Unlike Alberta's ATIA/POPA, FIPPA does not have a single hard deadline for Privacy Management Programs. The obligation to govern personal information — including AI exposure — is ongoing. A complaint does not require a missed deadline to succeed. The risk is present now.

What You Need to Build

The Core Compliance Components for Manitoba Municipalities

The following checklist represents the foundational governance that a Manitoba local public body needs to address AI-related FIPPA exposure. This is not an exhaustive privacy framework — it is the minimum necessary to govern AI use responsibly under the Act.

  • Designate a Privacy Officer

    Formally designate a staff member as Privacy Officer in writing. This person receives access requests, manages potential breaches, and maintains your privacy governance program. Document the designation in a council or administrative resolution.

  • Conduct a Personal Information Inventory

    Map every location where your municipality collects, uses, or discloses personal information — including AI tools staff are already using. Shadow AI tools belong in this inventory, even if they are not officially sanctioned.

  • Draft an AI Acceptable Use Policy

    Create a formal policy governing staff use of AI tools. Specify which tools are sanctioned, which are prohibited, what data staff may and may not enter, and the consequences of unauthorized use. This is the control that addresses shadow AI directly.

  • Establish Breach Response Procedures

    Document the steps your municipality will follow if a privacy breach occurs — who is notified, in what timeframe, and what records are kept. FIPPA requires notification procedures for breaches involving personal information.

  • Deliver and Document Staff Training

    Provide privacy and AI use training to all staff who handle personal information. Keep records of who was trained, when, and on what content. Training is not optional — it is a documented component of your privacy governance.

  • Review Vendor and Cloud Agreements

    Review every software contract and cloud service agreement involving personal information. Confirm that data processing agreements are in place and that vendors meet FIPPA requirements. Flag any AI vendors processing citizen data without an executed agreement.

  • Document and Maintain the Program

    Compile all of the above into a single governance document. Date it, sign it, and bring it to council. Establish a review cycle — at minimum annually. Update it as your technology stack and AI use evolves.

For Small Municipalities

One CAO. The Same Legal Obligation.

Larger Manitoba municipalities have in-house legal counsel, IT departments, and administrative capacity to address privacy governance internally. Small and rural municipalities — towns, rural municipalities, local urban districts — typically don't.

The FIPPA obligation applies regardless of size. A rural municipality with a CAO and four staff has the same legal obligation as Winnipeg — with a fraction of the internal capacity to address it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Manitoba FIPPA and AI — Common Questions

Does FIPPA apply to Manitoba municipalities?

Yes. FIPPA Part 2 applies to local public bodies, including municipalities. It governs how personal information is collected, used, disclosed, retained, and disposed of — including when AI tools are involved in any of those processes.

Who oversees FIPPA compliance for Manitoba municipalities?

The Manitoba Ombudsman investigates privacy complaints under FIPPA for local public bodies. Unlike most provinces, Manitoba does not have a dedicated Information and Privacy Commissioner. The Ombudsman has broad investigative powers and can report findings publicly.

Is there a specific FIPPA deadline for AI governance?

No single hard deadline exists under FIPPA for privacy management programs — unlike Alberta's ATIA/POPA. However, the obligation to govern personal information is ongoing and active. A complaint about unauthorized AI use of citizen data does not require a missed deadline to succeed.

Our staff use Microsoft 365 and Copilot is appearing in our tools. Do we need to act?

Yes. Microsoft Copilot integrated into 365 interacts with municipal data — including emails, documents, and records that may contain citizen personal information. Under FIPPA, using AI tools that process personal information without documented governance and vendor agreements creates compliance exposure. A Privacy Impact Assessment and updated vendor agreement are the starting points.
